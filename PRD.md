# The Voter Guider — Product Requirements Document

## Vision
Help voters make confident, informed decisions by surfacing who actually matches their values — not just who has the most name recognition.

## Problem
Most voter information tools are either too shallow (candidate bios) or too overwhelming (full policy documents). Voters in contested races — especially down-ballot — often skip candidates they don't recognize, or vote on party alone.

## Target user
A voter in Seattle, Fort Collins or Monterey Park preparing for the **November 3, 2026 general election**. Generalizes to any US voter with sufficient data.

---

## Core features

### 1. Voter Quiz (Decision Support)
**Goal:** Help a user discover which candidates match their values across ALL races on their ballot.

- Dynamic length: users answer as many questions as they want; results update live.
- Confidence-based stop suggestions: "Quick" (5q), "Confident" (12q / gap ≥ 0.25), "Thorough" (all / gap ≥ 0.8).
- Adaptive question ordering: next question chosen by expected information gain (stance variance × office uncertainty).
- Per-question importance weighting (0 = doesn't matter → 3 = very important).
- Fully transparent scoring: every point is traceable to (question → issue → candidate stance → weight × importance × agreement).
- Results show ranked candidates per office with an expandable breakdown table.

### 2. Candidate Review Pages
- Full profile: bio, party, incumbent status, photo, website.
- All stances with source citations.
- Links to their office page and the quiz.

### 3. Office Comparison Pages
- All candidates for a single office side-by-side.
- Stance grid: row = issue, column = candidate, cell = badge + 1-sentence summary.
- Quick-link to individual candidate profiles and back to the election.

### 4. Issue Pages
- Policy topic explainer (summary + longer description).
- All candidates across active elections ranked by stance.
- Visual spectrum from "Strongly Opposes" to "Strongly Supports".
- Source links for every stance.

### 5. Election Overview Page
- Date, jurisdiction, official site.
- All offices and their candidates.
- All ballot measures.
- Quiz CTA and issues sidebar.

### 6. Original Guide Builder (retained)
- Users can still manually track YES/NO/candidate picks with notes.
- Share guides via URL with a token.

## Data model highlights
See `CLAUDE.md` for the full schema. Key normalized tables: `Office`, `Candidate`, `Issue`, `CandidateStance`, `Source`, `Quiz`, `Question`, `QuestionIssue`, `QuestionOption`, `UserAnswer`.

### Generalization
The system is election-agnostic. Adding a new location requires only:
1. `data/elections/<slug>.json` — offices + candidates
2. `data/stances/<slug>.json` — stance data with sources
3. `data/quizzes/<slug>.json` — quiz questions
4. Re-running `npm run seed`

No code changes needed for new locations.

## November 3, 2026 General — current scope

Locations: Seattle (King County, WA), Fort Collins (Larimer County, CO), Monterey Park
(Los Angeles County, CA). Every federal, state, county and city contest and measure on
those ballots that official sources confirm, with a district picker for district races.

The earlier June 2026 California primary bundle was removed: its content was generated,
not researched (e.g. it listed a term-limited governor as a candidate). Rule going forward:
**no made-up data** — every ballot fact and every candidate position must be traceable to
a source recorded in `data/research/`.

## Stance authoring
Each quiz question is a single statement. A candidate's position on it (−2…+2) is included
only when the candidate or a cited source has stated it, and every stance stores the
verbatim supporting quote. Stances are generated by `data/research/quiz-builders/`, which
refuses to write a stance whose quote isn't found in the downloaded source.

Sources used for Nov 2026: CalMatters voter guide (CA), CPR News voter guide (CO),
Washington State voters' pamphlet candidate statements (WA).

## Scoring algorithm
```
For each candidate C in office O:
  score = Σ over answered questions Q:
    Σ over (issue I, weight W) linked to Q:
      if C has stance on I:
        agreement = 4 - |userStance - candidateStance|  // 0..4
        contribution = agreement × W × importance
        score += contribution
  normalized = score / maxPossible  // 0..1
```

Confidence per office:
```
gap = top_normalized - second_normalized
depthFactor = min(answeredCount / 10, 1)
confidence = min(gap × 2.5 × depthFactor, 1)
```

## Not in scope (v1)
- Admin UI for stance editing
- Additional locations beyond Seattle, Fort Collins and Monterey Park
- Automated stance extraction from news/documents
- Automated ballot ingestion (ballots are curated from official sources)
- Real-time election results / ballot tracking
- Mobile app

## Backlog

Stories are in priority order inside each group. Every story inherits the rules in `CLAUDE.md`:
each fact shows its source beside it, nothing is made up, and any rule applies to everyone in a race
or to every measure alike. Last updated 2026-10-30.

### Now: before November 3, 2026

**V-1. Short clips for every candidate who has a video**
As a voter with a few minutes per contest, I want a bite or snack of each candidate before anything long.
- Each candidate with a long independent video gets their opening statement or first full answer as a clip.
- Times come from a transcript; no transcript or no such answer means no clip, never an estimate.
- Open: about a dozen candidates still waiting on captions (YouTube rate limit), and Tatiana Brown, who gave no opening in her forum.

**V-2. Interviews lead over campaign cuts**
As a voter, I want to see candidates questioned by someone independent before I see their own ad.
- The 7 candidates whose only short video is a campaign cut, but who have a longer independent video, lead with a clip of it.
- Candidates with no independent video keep the campaign cut, labeled as one.

**V-3. Video coverage gaps**
As a voter, I want to see and hear the people on my ballot.
- Run the same search for every candidate in a race, and record it in the picks files.
- Today: WA 45 of 75 candidates, CO 30 of 50, CA 31 of 77.
- No "no video found" placeholders.

**M-1. More sourced dollar figures for stakes**
As a voter, I want a measure's stakes grade to rest on the best official number available.
- Every measure either has an official figure with its quote, or says none is available.
- Figures from PDFs get saved source text so the test can check the quote, as bundle text already does.
- Open: the Monterey Park, Montebello and LA County measures, and Seattle Prop 1.

### Next: quality of what voters see

**F-1. Poop meter from several fact-checkers**
As a voter, I want the 💩 meter to rest on more than PolitiFact.
- Add sources whose `robots.txt` allows Claude-User: FactCheck.org, Snopes, Lead Stories, Washington Post, 9NEWS. AP and Reuters disallow it. CalMatters returned 403 on the check.
- Add the Google Fact Check Tools API if a key is provided.
- Each outlet's verdict maps to one shared scale, published on `/how-we-score`; a claim checked by several outlets counts once.
- Show a range and a claim count, not one precise-looking percent, and keep a minimum number of claims.
- Say plainly that it measures the share of checked claims, because fact-checkers choose what to check.

**E-1. Endorsements for the rest of California and Colorado**
As a voter, I want to see who backs each candidate, not only in Washington and the LA Sheriff race.
- Same method as WA: each campaign's own page, saved, with every name checked against it.
- Never say "no endorsements"; a campaign may just not publish a list we can read.

**E-2. Comparison sources for Washington and Colorado**
As a voter, I want Compare view to be as useful in every state.
- Add published guides where `robots.txt` allows them, party and advocacy guides as left/right pairs.

**D-1. Debates for every state measure**
As a voter, I want to hear both sides of a measure argued.
- Follow the Prop 45 pattern: shortest first, each video labeled by who made it.

**J-1. Judicial records**
As a voter, I want something real to go on for judges.
- Add rulings or opinions only where a source allows it (courts.ca.gov lists about 120 days; CourtListener blocks Claude-User).

**C-1. Compare view on Print and export**
As a voter, I want to take a side-by-side with me.
- Printing Compare view gives a readable table, marked as not a ballot, with sources.

### Later: after the election

**L-2. Next election cycle**

**Q-1. Share quiz results**
As a voter, I want a public link to my quiz breakdown that I can send to someone.

As a voter, I want a candidate page to carry the same clips, stakes-style clarity and cleanup as the Expanded view.

### Won't do
- Tell voters how to vote, or rank measures by whether we think they're good.
- Use sources whose terms or `robots.txt` block us: VOTE411 stays link-only; Ballotpedia blocks automated access.
- Show an inferred stance or a score without enough evidence.
